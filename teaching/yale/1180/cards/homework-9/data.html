Problems 5–8 concern the following situation. Suppose when pizzas sell for $p$ dollars each, New Haven residents want to buy

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}q = 10 - p + d\end{alignat*}$$

thousand pizzas per week, and pizza restaurants want to sell

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}q = p - 2 + s\end{alignat*}$$

thousand pizzas per week. The market settles at the equilibrium price, i.e. the one where these two expressions for $q$ are equal. The variables $d$ and $s$ are called shocks. If $d > 0$, buyers demand $d$ thousand more pizzas than usual, regardless of price (for example, because a new restaurant opened). If $s > 0$, restaurants are willing to sell $s$ thousand more pizzas, regardless of price. Both $d$ and $s$ can be negative.

5. Find the equilibrium price $p(d, s)$ and quantity $q(d, s)$ of pizzas sold (in thousands), both in terms of $d$ and $s$.

6. Let $T : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be given by

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}T\left( \left[\begin{array}{c} d \\ s \end{array}\right] \right) = \left[ \begin{array}{c} p(d, s) - p(0, 0) \\ q(d, s) - q(0, 0) \end{array} \right],\end{alignat*}$$

i.e. the change in the equilibrium price and quantity when there are shocks $d$ and $s$. Find the matrix $A$ for $T$, and then find $A^{-1}$.

7. Economists typically can’t observe shocks directly, only prices and quantities. If the price of pizza in New Haven rose by $\$1$ last week and the number of pizzas sold fell by 3 thousand, what shocks occurred?

8. Suppose you only have data on quantities, not prices. Let $Q : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}Q\left( \left[\begin{array}{c} d \\ s \end{array}\right] \right) = q(d, s) - q(0, 0),\end{alignat*}$$

so its matrix is just the second row of the matrix for $T$. Find a basis for $\ker Q$. Explain in context what the kernel means, and specifically why it is a problem for determining shocks.
