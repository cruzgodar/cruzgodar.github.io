Problems 10–12 concern the following situation. Suppose that one year from now, the economy will be in one of three states: a boom, a normal year, or a bust. You can buy three kinds of assets right now, each of which pays a different amount in each situation next year:

• A bond costs $\$95$ today and pays $\$100$ next year no matter what.

• A share of stock costs $\$180$ today and pays $\$400$ in a boom, $\$200$ in a normal year, and nothing in a bust.

• An insurance contract costs $\$125$ today and pays nothing in a boom, $\$100$ in a normal year, and $\$300$ in a bust.

You can buy any amount of each asset, including fractions. Negative values in a portfolio are allowed and mean short selling: for example, buying $-1$ bond is the same as borrowing $\$95$ today and paying back $\$100$ next year.

10. Suppose you buy $b$ bonds, $s$ stocks, and $c$ insurance contracts, and

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}\vec{x} = \left[\begin{array}{c} b \\ s \\ c \end{array}\right]\end{alignat*}$$

is a vector representing your portfolio. Find a matrix $A$ so that $A\vec{x}$ is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose components are your total payouts (i.e. revenue, not profit) next year in each of the three scenarios.

11. A bank wants to sell a new asset that pays $\$300$ in a boom, $\$100$ in a normal year, and nothing in a bust. Find the portfolio $\vec{x}$ that pays exactly the same as this new asset in every scenario; this is called a replicating portfolio. How much does the replicating portfolio cost today?

12. Suppose the bank doesn’t do this work and prices the new asset at $\$100$ instead. Describe a set of trades that gives you money today and leaves you owing nothing next year, no matter which state happens (called an arbitrage). What do you expect will happen to the price of the new asset as people take advantage of it?
