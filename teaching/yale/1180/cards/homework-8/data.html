Problems 12–15 concern the following situation. A beekeeper keeps $h$ beehives next to an orchard with $a$ acres of apple trees, and the two businesses help one another synergistically. For the amount of production these businesses are considering, the function $T$ sending $\left[\begin{array}{c} h \\ a \end{array}\right]$ to $\left[\begin{array}{c} \text{pounds of honey} \\ \text{bushels of apples} \end{array}\right]$ is a linear transformation.

12. For these two businesses, $10$ hives and $20$ acres produce $500$ pounds of honey and $1040$ bushels of apples, and $20$ hives and $10$ acres produce $700$ pounds of honey and $580$ bushels of apples. Use this to find the matrix for $T$ and interpret each of its four entries in context.

13. The columns of the matrix for $T$ form a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$ (you don’t need to show this). The two businesses would like to produce exactly $1000$ pounds of honey and $2080$ bushels of apples next year. Express that vector in this basis, and interpret what the two coefficients mean.

14. Honey sells for $\$5$ per pound and apples for $\$20$ per bushel. Since expanding operations means using less ideal terrain, maintaining $h$ hives costs the beekeeper $5h^2$ dollars per year, and maintaining $a$ acres costs the apple farmer $5a^2$ dollars per year. Find the beekeeper’s profit $\pi_B(h, a)$ and the apple farmer’s profit $\pi_A(h, a)$. Suppose the beekeeper maximizes $\pi_B(h, a)$ and uses the value of $h$ to determine the amount of hives to keep, and the apple farmer maximizes $\pi_A(h, a)$ and uses the value of $a$ to determine the amount of acres to plant. Find the total profit of both businesses.

15. Find the values of $h$ and $a$ that maximize the combined profit $\pi_B + \pi_A$, using the second derivative test to verify it’s a maximum.
