1. Let $f(x, y) = \dfrac{e^{x - y}}{y}$. Compute $\nabla\! f$ and $D_{\vec{u}}f(3, 1)$, where $\vec{u} = \left< \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \right>$.

2. Let $g(x, y, z) = x^2 + 2y^2 + \sin(z)$. Compute $\nabla\! g$ and $D_{\vec{u}}g(1, -1, 0)$, where $\vec{u}$ points in the direction of $\left< 1, 1, 1 \right>$.

3. Let $f(x, y)$ be a function so that $D_{\vec{u}}f(4, 5) = 5$ and $D_{\vec{v}}f(4, 5) = 4$, where $\vec{u}$ points in the same direction as $\left< 1, 2 \right>$ and $\vec{v} = \left< \frac{3}{5}, \frac{4}{5} \right>. What is $\G f (4, 5)$?

4. Let $f(x, y) = x + y^2 + e^{x^2 + y^2}$. Find the equation for the tangent line to the graph of $f$ at $(2, 3)$ in the direction $\left< -1, -1 \right>$.

5. Let $f(x, y)$ be a function differentiable at $(a, b)$, and let $\vec{g} = \left< 1, 1, f_x(a, b) + f_y(a, b) \right>$. If the tangent plane to $f$ at $(a, b)$ has normal vector $\vec{n}$, what is $\vec{n} \bullet \vec{g}$? Explain.

6. A variable $w$ is given by $w = f(x, y, z)$, where $x = x(a)$, $y = y(a, b, c)$, and $z = z(x, a, c)$.

a) Express $\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}$ using the multivariable Chain Rule.

b) If $\frac{\partial w}{\partial c} = 2$, $\frac{\partial w}{\partial y} = 1$, $\frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 3$, and $\frac{\partial z}{\partial c} = -4$, what is $\frac{\partial y}{\partial c}$?

7. It’s a foggy day on the $xy$-plane. The density of the fog at the point $(x, y)$ is given in milligrams per cubic meter by the function

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}f(x,y) = x^3 - 4xy + 2y^2 + 5.\end{alignat*}$$

a) If you move in the direction of $\vec{v}= \left< -3,4 \right>$ from the point $(1,-2)$, what is the instantaneous rate of change of the fog?

b) In what direction should you move from $(1,-2)$ to decrease the density of the fog the fastest. Express your answer as a unit vector.

c) Find a direction in which you can move from the point $(1,-2)$ in order to keep the density of the fog exactly the same. Express your answer as a unit vector.

Problems 8–11 concern the following situation. Steep cafe finds that the number of cups of cold brew it sells per week is

$$\begin{alignat*}{99}C(p, l, s) = \frac{900\sqrt{l}}{p + s},\end{alignat*}$$

where $p$ is Steep’s price for a cup of cold brew, $s$ is its price for a scone, and $l$ is the price of a canned latte from the nearby cooler, all in dollars. Right now, $p = 4$, $l = 4$, and $s = 2$. Two goods are called substitutes if raising the price of one increases the demand for the other, and complements if raising the price of one decreases the demand for the other (we explored this briefly in past problem sets).

8. Compute $C$ and all of its partial derivatives at these current prices, including units. Are canned lattes and cold brew substitutes or complements? What about scones and cold brew?

9. Suppose all three prices are changing: the price of a cup of cold brew is increasing at $\$0.10$ per month, the price of a canned latte is increasing at $\$0.40$ per month (sadly), and the price of a scone is decreasing at $\$0.05$ per month. How fast is Steep’s weekly sales of cold brew increasing? Why is it increasing even though cold brew is getting more expensive?

10. Both the cold brew and scones use milk, so Steep sets their prices based on the wholesale price $m$ of a gallon of milk: $p = 2 + m$ and $s = 1 + \frac{m}{2}$. Currently, $m = 2$. Draw a tree diagram for $C$ as a function of $m$ and $t$, and use it to compute $\frac{\partial C}{\partial m}$. Interpret your answer in context.

11. On average, Steep sells one scone for every two cups of coffee, so it sells $S = \frac{C}{2}$ scones per week, and its total weekly revenue is $R = pC + sS$. Draw a tree diagram for $R$, where $R$ depends directly on $p$, $C$, $s$, and $S$, and use it to compute $\frac{\partial R}{\partial p}$ and $\frac{\partial R}{\partial s}$ at the current prices. Stores sometimes deliberately set a low price on one item to bring in customers; that item is called a loss leader. Should Steep consider making its scones a loss leader?
