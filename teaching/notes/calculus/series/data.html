Section 4.2: Series

With a strong foundation of sequences built up, let’s turn to their main application in this course: infinite series. Where a sequence is an infinite list of numbers, a series is an infinite sum. Keep in mind the course roadmap: eventually, we’ll want to add up not just numbers, but monomials, forming an object that’s something like a polynomial with infinitely many terms. To start with, though, we’ll stick to numbers only.

The purpose of partial sums is to let us talk about the convergence of series. The behavior of the terms we’re adding up turns out not to be particularly helpful for this: if we want to assign a value to , then knowing that doesn’t tell us much. In fact, if we’re adding up infinitely many positive things, those things had better converge to zero — if they don’t, there’s no chance the sum will be finite. What we want is the limit of a sequence that gives better and better approximations of the infinite sum, and that’s exactly what the partial sums do.
