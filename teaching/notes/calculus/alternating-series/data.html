Series that converge conditionally have the unfortunate property that they can be rearranged to converge to any real number, or to diverge. Absolutely convergent series, on the other hand, are resilient to rearrangement — they will always converge to the same value, no matter the order of their terms. The names do a good job of describing the definitions: absolutely convergent series are those whose series of absolute values converge, while conditionally convergent series are conditional on not being rearranged to converge.
