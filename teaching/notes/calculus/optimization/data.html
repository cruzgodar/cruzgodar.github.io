Section 2.8: Optimization

With implicit differentiation behind us, we can move on to the applications of differentiation: not just what they can do in terms of slopes, but also other graph features. We’ll begin with arguably the most useful thing derivatives are used for: optimizing functions.

The idea is to figure out when a function is as large or as small as possible. That can mean maximizing profit, minimizing cost, or just about anything else. Let’s give these ideas names so they’re a little easier to talk about.

Definition: inflection point

Let be a function. An inflection point of is an -value where and changes sign at .

At critical points, a function often switches from increasing to decreasing or vice versa (not always — remember that saddle points exist). Similarly, at inflection points, functions switch from an increasing derivative to a decreasing one, or vice versa. You’ll hear the term inflection point commonly used in the media — of late, it’s often used to describe waves of case numbers for diseases. The inflection point on the beginning of a wave is the point where the derivative stops increasing and starts to decrease. Remember that we can interpret the derivative as approximately the change in the function value when the input changes by : if is the number of new cases on day , then is approximately the amount that that number increases between days and . Therefore, the inflection point on the front side of a wave is the first day on which the number of new cases jumps up by more than it jumped up the day before.

Theorem: the First Derivative Test

Let be a differentiable function and write down all of its critical points from left to right. Take a critical point and evaluate at two places: in between and the next critical point to the left and in between and the next critical point to the right. If the derivative switches sign from positive to negative, then is a local maximum. If it switches sign from negative to positive, then is a local minimum. If it doesn’t switch sign, then is a saddle point.
