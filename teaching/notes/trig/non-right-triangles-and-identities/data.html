Non-Right Triangles

With the ability to solve trig equations, we can approach another application of trig functions: their use in non-right triangles. Our ability to use trig functions in right triangles came from the unit circle, as with most things, and so to approach non-right triangles, we’ll place them in the unit circle too.

Right triangles have a lot of convenient structure that leads to straightforward terminology: once we’ve chosen an angle, we can label the sides as opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse. The Law of Sines neatly addresses both of the cases that the Law of Cosines misses: if we know two sides and an angle not between them, then that angle is opposite one of the sides, so the Law of Sines lets us solve for the sine of the other angle. And if we know two angles and a side, then we actually know all three angles since they have to sum to , and so we can solve for both the remaining sides. If all we know is all three angles, we can’t solve for any sides because of similar triangles: scaling a triangle up changes all of its side lengths but leaves the angles the same, so knowing only the angles of a triangle doesn’t determine its side lengths.
